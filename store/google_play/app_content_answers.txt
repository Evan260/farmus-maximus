FARMUS MAXIMUS: ANSWERS FOR PLAY CONSOLE'S APP CONTENT FORMS

Play Console > Policy and programs > App content (some help pages call it Policy > App content). The forms are below
in the order Play Console lists them, each with the answer to click and why. They were checked against Google's help
pages on 24 November 2026. Play Console words some questions differently from the help pages, and it changes them now
and then, so where a form reads differently, answer by the meaning given here.

What the answers rest on, from the code and the built bundle (builds/android/Farmus Maximus.aab):
- The game itself sends nothing anywhere: no HTTP, sockets, analytics, crash reporting or ads anywhere in its code.
- Saves, settings and whether the full game is owned are files on the phone (user://).
- Google Play Billing sells the one product, full_game. The game asks Play only for the price and whether it is owned.
- Play Games: achievements through Play Games Services v2, with a sign-in offered once. It only runs in a build whose
  Android presets have slowsunday_phone/play_games_app_id set. Both presets leave it blank today, so the current
  bundle has Play Games switched off (the plugin removes its start-up provider from the manifest).
- Reminders are local notifications, set with Android's ordinary alarms and shown by the phone; nothing goes over the
  network. The game asks for the notification permission only when the player taps Allow in Settings > Notifications.
- Permissions in the bundle's manifest: INTERNET and ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE (from the Play Billing Library),
  com.android.vending.BILLING, POST_NOTIFICATIONS and RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED (from the SlowSunday phone plugin), and
  the app's own DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION (from AndroidX). No AD_ID.


1. PRIVACY POLICY

Answer: the address of privacy.html once it is published. Fill it in later.
Why: every app needs one, even one that collects nothing. It must be a public web page, not a PDF, and must name the
developer shown on the listing; the page names Slow Sunday Games.
Also needed: Google wants the policy linked inside the game too, and the game has no such link yet. A "Privacy
policy" button in Settings that opens the page meets Google's rule and Apple's.


2. APP ACCESS (the help pages now call this "Sign-in details")

Answer: All functionality in my app is available without any special access.
Why: there is no login. The full game is unlocked by an ordinary Google Play purchase, which is not an account, and
the optional Play Games sign-in changes nothing in play.


3. ADS

Answer: No, my app does not contain ads.
Why: no ads of any kind, and no ad library in the bundle.

5. TARGET AUDIENCE AND CONTENT

Target age groups: 13-15, 16-17 and 18 and over.
Why: a relaxed estate-management game with a lot of reading, built for teenagers and adults. Leaving out the under-13
groups keeps the game outside Google's Families policy, which binds every app with any child age group selected.
If Play asks whether the store listing could unintentionally appeal to children: No.
  Its characters are adults on a Roman estate. If a reviewer still finds the pets or the art aimed at children,
  Google's help page says the way out is to change those store pictures or to add the child age groups and follow
  the Families policy.

What choosing an under-13 group would bring in, under the Families policy:
- The game and every library in it must meet COPPA, GDPR and the other children's privacy laws.
- Only APIs and SDKs approved for child-directed use. Play Games sign-in would need checking against Google's terms
  for child-directed apps before it could stay; Google Play Billing is fine.
- No advertising ID, device identifiers or location from children (the game already sends none).
- Purchases must make clear that they cost real money (the Unlock button already shows the store's own price).
- Ads would have to come from a Families self-certified ads SDK (moot, as there are none).
- The content must suit the youngest group chosen, the privacy policy must cover children, and the Data safety form
  must say what is collected from them.
- Google reviews the audience claim, which can take 7 days or longer, and checks every update against these rules.
- In return the game could be considered for the Teacher Approved program and the Kids tab.

8. DATA SAFETY

The answers depend on whether the uploaded bundle has Play Games switched on.

A. A bundle without the Play Games app id (the current one)

Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? No.
Why: the game sends nothing off the phone, and what it knows about the purchase stays in a file on the phone, which
is not "collection" in Google's sense. The Play Billing Library talks to Google itself, and Google's Data safety help
says that for a payment service such as Google Play's billing system "you don't need to declare collection of the
data that the payment service collects in connection with its processing of financial transactions", as long as the
app never sees that data and the service collects it under its own terms, which is the case here.
Play then skips the other questions, and the listing reads "No data collected" and "No data shared with third
parties".

B. A bundle with the Play Games app id set

Data collection and security
- Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? Yes.
  Play Games, a library in the game, sends data to Google, and Google counts what an app's libraries send.
- Is all of the user data collected by your app encrypted in transit? Yes.
  Google's Play Games data page says it encrypts everything it collects in transit with HTTPS.
- If asked how users can create an account: My app does not allow users to create an account.
  A Play Games profile is the player's own Google profile, made and kept by Google, not an account with Slow Sunday
  Games.
- Do you provide a way for users to request that their data is deleted? Yes.
  The privacy policy tells players to delete their Play Games profile and its game data in the Play Games app or
  their Google Account, and to email for anything else.

Data types: tick these four, and nothing else.
- Personal info > User IDs: the Play Games player ID and gamer name.
- App activity > App interactions: the analytics Play Games collects on its own.
- App activity > Other actions: the achievements earned (Google's examples for this type include gameplay).
- App info and performance > Diagnostics: the diagnostics Play Games collects on its own.

Data usage and handling, for each type:
- User IDs: collected; not shared; not processed ephemerally; optional (the player chooses to sign in);
  purpose App functionality.
- Other actions: collected; not shared; not processed ephemerally; optional; purpose App functionality.
- App interactions: collected; not shared; not processed ephemerally; required (Play Games collects it on its own
  whenever it runs); purpose Analytics.
- Diagnostics: collected; not shared; not processed ephemerally; required; purpose Analytics.
"Not shared" because the game passes nothing to anyone: Google collects this as the Play Games service the player
signs in to, which is how Google's own Play Games data page describes it
(developer.android.com/games/pgs/data-collection).

HOW FARMUS DIFFERS FROM THE OTHER THREE

- The only one with reminders, and the only one that will turn on Play Games. Once its app id is set, its Data
  safety answers change to part B; the other three always answer "No data collected".
- Alcohol references (wine) in the rating, as in Mosaicus. Fishus has none, and Legionus one line of history.
- Rated about Everyone, like Fishus and Mosaicus. Legionus has combat and blood and rates about Teen.

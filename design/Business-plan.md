# Farmus Maximus: Business Plan

## The game

A cozy, semi-idle Roman farming game. Grow lavender fields, sell your harvest and restore a ruined farmhouse into a grand villa. Play actively or let it run along the bottom of your screen.

**Hook:** Rusty's Retirement meets Cast n Chill, on a Roman villa.

## Where it sells

- **Steam first**, for Windows, Mac and Linux, with Steam Deck support.
- **Phones second:** Android and iOS, a few months after the Steam launch (see below).
- **Price:** \$10 to \$15.
- **No own website or payment system.** The Steam page is the home of the game.

## Who it is for

Players of cozy, idle and desktop games (Cast n Chill, Rusty's Retirement, Stardew Valley), and people who like to keep something calm running while they work.

## Plan

1. **Prototype** the home farm (1 to 2 weeks).
2. **Put up the Steam page** with real screenshots and a trailer.
3. **Post short clips** on TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Reddit, all linking to Steam.
4. **Checkpoint** (see below).
5. **Polish** the game.
6. **Free demo** for Steam Next Fest.
7. **Launch** on Steam.
8. **Phone version** on Google Play and the App Store.

## Checkpoint

After the Steam page and 3 months of clips:

| Wishlists | Decision |
|---|---|
| Under 1,000 | Change the hook or the art, or stop |
| 1,000 to 7,000 | Keep going, keep posting |
| 7,000 to 10,000+ by launch | On track for a solid launch |

## The Steam page and clips

- **Trailer:** 30 to 60 seconds; the first 5 seconds show the lavender, the villa and the dog.
- **Store page:** capsule images, at least 5 in-game screenshots (Steam expects the real game, not concept art), the description, and the tags Cozy, Idler, Farming Sim, Relaxing, Pixel Graphics, Simulation and Casual.
- **Clips,** 3 to 5 a week, recorded with Godot's movie mode:
  - wind rolling through the lavender at golden hour
  - the villa going from ruin to the next stage
  - the game running as a strip along the bottom of a real work desktop
  - petting the dog
  - a full day passing in 15 seconds
  - a rare sighting: the fox, the hoopoe, a shooting star
  - day 1 next to day 30 of the estate

## Steam's timing

- A new developer account has a waiting period after paying the fee before its first release.
- The "Coming soon" page must be live at least two weeks before launch.
- Next Fest runs three times a year (February, June and October). Games whose demo was out a few months before it got about 2.5 times the median wishlists from the festival.

## Before launch

- Playtest with 10 to 20 outside players and fix the confusing spots and the pacing.
- A performance pass on low-end laptops and the Steam Deck, then the Steam Deck Verified submission.
- Decide the launch discount.
- Send keys to small and mid-sized cozy and idle game creators.
- A press kit: logo, key art, screenshots, trailer and a fact sheet in a shared Google Drive folder.

## Money

- Steam keeps 30%. On a \$10 game, about **\$6 to \$7 per copy** reaches us after Steam's cut, taxes and refunds.
- **Break even:** roughly 300 to 500 copies.
- **Good result:** 5,000 to 15,000 copies (\$30,000 to \$100,000).

## Extra income

- **Supporter pack:** a cheap add-on for players who want to give more; about 1 in 10 Rusty's Retirement buyers bought one.
- **Soundtrack:** sold as an add-on, as competitors do.
- **Free seasonal content after launch,** built around the Roman festivals, to bring players back.

## Phones (Android and iOS)

The game engine (Godot) builds for both, and a semi-idle game with offline progress suits phones well: players check in a few times a day.

- **When:** after the Steam launch, so the phone version gets the finished, reviewed game and the Steam launch keeps all the attention.
- **Price:** free to download with the home farm and the olive grove (the same limit as the Steam demo), then **one purchase of about \$5 to \$7** unlocks the whole estate. No ads and no random rewards, which keeps the game cozy and matches the Steam version.
- **Store cut:** both stores keep 15% under their small business programs (the first \$1 million a year), so about **\$4 to \$5 per unlock** reaches us before taxes.
- **Work needed:**
  - Touch controls: tapping and dragging already work; digging up a plant needs a long press instead of a right-click.
  - Larger text and buttons for small screens (the interface size setting is the starting point).
  - Saving whenever the app is put away, since phones close apps without warning.
  - Phone notifications for "the cart is back" and "the storehouse is full".
  - Achievements and cloud saves through Google Play Games and Apple Game Center instead of Steam.
  - The desktop strip does not exist on phones; the game simply runs full screen.
- **Note:** building for iOS needs a Mac with Xcode.
- **Risk:** the phone stores are crowded and mostly free-to-play games with ads, so a paid unlock depends on the same clips and wishlist audience that sells the Steam version.

## Only if it sells very well

- A proper website.
- Console versions (Nintendo Switch and others) through a porting partner or publisher.
- More lands and content updates.
- Other stores such as GOG or itch.io.
